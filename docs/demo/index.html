Free Coffee and the Length of Meetings

A. Nonymous

Abstract

This paper examines whether a free coffee machine changes how long staff meetings run. A fictional office of 120 people received a machine in half of its meeting rooms. Meetings in rooms with a machine ran 7 minutes shorter, off a base of 52 minutes. Attendance was unchanged.

Background

Meeting length is usually treated as a function of the agenda rather than of the room (Doe 2020). Roe and Poe (2019) instead argue that small frictions in the room set the pace, and that anything reducing the cost of staying alert should shorten the meeting rather than lengthen it. The two accounts predict opposite signs, so a coffee machine is a clean test. Section 2 describes the office, the assignment of machines to rooms, and the meetings we observe.

Data and design

We observe 480 invented meetings over one invented quarter, in an office where half of the meeting rooms were assigned a machine at random. Table 1 reports the means.

Results

Meetings in a room with a machine ran 6.9 minutes shorter, a decline of 13 percent against the 52 minute base in Table 1. Attendance moved by 0.1 people and is a precise zero. The estimate is β̂=−6.9\hat{\beta} = -6.9 with a standard error of 1.41.4.

We read this as support for the account in Roe and Poe (2019), with one limit: rooms were assigned the machine, and people chose the room, so a team that already ran short meetings may have booked its way into treatment (Smith 2021).

\begin{abstract}
This paper examines whether a free coffee machine changes how long staff
meetings run. A fictional office of 120 people received a machine in half of
its meeting rooms. Meetings in rooms with a machine ran 7 minutes shorter, off
a base of 52 minutes. Attendance was unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Meeting length is usually treated as a function of the agenda rather than of
the room \citep{doe2020example}. \citet{roe2019placeholder} instead argue that
small frictions in the room set the pace, and that anything reducing the cost
of staying alert should shorten the meeting rather than lengthen it. The two
accounts predict opposite signs, so a coffee machine is a clean test.

\section{Data and design}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Meetings in a room with a machine ran 6.9 minutes shorter, a decline of 13
percent against the 52 minute base in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Attendance moved by
0.1 people and is a precise zero. The estimate is
$\hat{\beta} = -6.9$ with a standard error of $1.4$.

We read this as support for the account in \citet{roe2019placeholder}, with one
limit: rooms were assigned the machine, and people chose the room, so a team
that already ran short meetings may have booked its way into treatment
\citep{smith2021fictitious}.
